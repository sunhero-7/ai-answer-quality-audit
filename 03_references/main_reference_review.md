## MP03 Two percentage changes use different bases

### Evidence

### Interpretation boundaries

## MP04 A combined mean with one group size missing

In a fictional puzzle trial, Group A contains 12 participants and has an exact mean completion time of 8 minutes. Group B has an exact mean completion time of 11 minutes, but its number of participants is missing; it contains at least one participant. Each participant completed the same puzzle once.

### Expected answer

It equals 9.5 minutes when n = 12, meaning the two groups have equal numbers of participants. The missing information is Group B's participant count; averaging the two means equally otherwise gives each group equal weight rather than each person.

### Checklist

- Give (96 + 11n)/(12 + n) minutes, or an algebraically equivalent weighted-mean formula.
- Explain that 9.5 minutes is correct if Group B also contains 12 participants.
- Identify Group B's participant count as missing.
- Keep the reply within 120 words and do not invent a group size.

### Evidence

- Group A has 12 participants with mean 8, hence total completion time 96 minutes.
- A mean of 11 for n participants implies total time 11n minutes.
- Solve 96 + 11n = 9.5(12 + n): 1.5n = 18, giving n = 12.
- Counterexample: if n = 4, combined mean is (96 + 44)/16 = 8.75 minutes, demonstrating that 9.5 is not forced.

### Interpretation boundaries

## MP08 Drawing counters without replacement

A bag contains four blue counters and two orange counters. Each individual counter is equally likely to be selected. Two counters are drawn one at a time without replacement.

Find the probability that both are blue and the probability that at least one is orange. Give each as a simplified fraction and a percentage, and show your reasoning. Explain briefly why the second blue probability is not 4/6 after a blue counter has been drawn first.

### Evidence

### Interpretation boundaries
