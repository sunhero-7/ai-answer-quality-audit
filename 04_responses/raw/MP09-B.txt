Using \(\Delta T=Q/(mc)\):

- Beaker A: \(\Delta T_A=4200/(0.50\times4200)=2.0^\circ\text{C}\).
- Beaker B: \(\Delta T_B=4200/(1.00\times4200)=1.0^\circ\text{C}\).

A’s temperature rises more, but this does **not** establish that it ends hotter. Their final temperatures are \(T_{A,i}+2.0^\circ\text{C}\) and \(T_{B,i}+1.0^\circ\text{C}\).

We need their initial temperatures, or at least the difference between them. A ends hotter if its initial temperature is less than \(1.0^\circ\text{C}\) below B’s, equal to B’s, or above B’s. If A starts exactly \(1.0^\circ\text{C}\) cooler, they finish at the same temperature.
