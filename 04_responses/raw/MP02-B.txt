The statement is right if “less effort” means a smaller pushing force, but wrong if it means less mechanical energy transferred.

At constant speed, the push balances gravity’s component down the slope: \(F=mg(h/L)\). The 4-metre ramp therefore needs half the force of the 2-metre ramp.

However, the worker pushes through twice the distance. The work done is \(W=FL=mgh\), so both routes transfer the same energy and produce the same gain in gravitational potential energy. The trolley’s kinetic energy stays constant.

At the same speed along each ramp, the longer ramp takes twice as long and requires half the mechanical power.

“Effort” could also mean fatigue or the worker’s bodily energy expenditure. Those depend on human physiology and cannot be determined from this idealised mechanical model.
